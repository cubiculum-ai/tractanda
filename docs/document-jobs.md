# Durable paged document jobs

The shared Coordinator dispatches the eight job methods through one sequential producer, resumes acknowledged frontiers at startup, and drains its owned process/material work before shutdown or index maintenance. A job publishes its canonical document representation and the derived index projection as part of completion. Current feature declarations, producer readiness and current permissions determine whether a connected client can start or recover a job.

`tractanda.document-jobs.v1` means the Coordinator job router is installed. It is independent of converter readiness: current authorized status/list/cancel/discard remains available when no converter is configured. Starting or resuming requires the operator-configured producer. Requests never supply executable/model paths. `DocumentJobResources` controls independent inspection, model-load, per-page, no-progress and termination-grace budgets; changing resource budgets does not change immutable extraction identity. Whole-document runtime is not bounded by the old aggregate converter timeout.

Operational defaults are generous on both macOS and Linux, and operators can tighten or extend them for their hardware. Inspection permits 30 minutes, model loading one hour, each page six hours, counter-based no-progress 30 minutes, and termination grace 15 seconds. These are independent finite bounds; a many-page job is not forced through one aggregate document deadline. Page size, tile count and generated text can affect processing time. Existing input, geometry, token, memory, output, staged-byte and free-space limits remain separate.

Model loading, rendering, preparation and prefill can perform legitimate CPU work without emitting a counter. Those validated phases use their fixed absolute phase deadline; missing counters do not invent a heartbeat or extend that deadline. During phases with meaningful counters, repeated or unchanged progress does not renew liveness. Cancellation and process-group cleanup still apply, and accepted page checkpoints remain available for resume.

The daemon accepts `--document-inspect-seconds`, `--document-model-load-seconds`, `--document-page-seconds`, `--document-no-progress-seconds` and `--document-termination-grace-seconds`. The same five values are available in the optional operator `DaemonConfiguration.documentJobResources` object. All values must be finite, positive and safely convertible; termination grace is at most 60 seconds. Changing these operational limits does not change extraction identity. Client requests cannot set executable/model paths or these operator limits.

Observed Linux CPU latency is substantial: the selected pinned model completed a 106-token synthetic PNG in 369 seconds, and two small PDF worker pages in about 374 seconds each, including approximately 361 seconds of silent prefill. The two PDFs matched their exact frozen table matrices and reading order. This is bounded fixture evidence, not a general throughput or recognition-quality guarantee; the known merged-header limitation remains a diagnostic failure. Much slower hardware can require larger configured budgets. Status, cancel and resume remain available while the asynchronous job processes pages.


`TractandaDocumentJob` defines `start`, `status`, bounded pending `list`, `cancel`, `resume`, deliberate `rebase`, provisional `page` and explicit `discard`. Start accepts item, expected revision, attachment and operation identities. A repeated actor/operation returns the same durable job or its reconciled commit. Status carries checked Int64 page counts and processing coverage; page count is absent before inspection. Completion means authorized item CAS after terminal coverage of every inspected page, independently of OCR accuracy.

An immutable private intent binds UUIDv1 job identity, original actor UID/principal, source descriptor/provenance, converter identity, configuration digest and extraction date. A bounded16KiB atomic checkpoint records publication expectation, generation fence, processing state/counters and a prepared tree or compact commit receipt. It contains no original bytes, document text, page arrays or raw stderr. Up to64 active/recovery jobs and eight pending jobs per actor are admitted; shared staged-byte quota and filesystem reserve remain independent. Completed/discarded compact records and actor/operation digest lookups archive durably before their active slots are released. This bounds pending work without imposing a lifetime job count. Terminal archives preserve no page/body payload.

Each accepted page is validated, sealed and synchronized before its checkpoint acknowledgement. Recovery streams accepted pages one at a time, rebuilding at most128 hash entries per level, and recognizes at most one sealed page beyond the acknowledged frontier. Exact page/node retries repair directory synchronization and reject changed bytes. Cancellation fences producers and retains accepted outcomes; discard records its tombstone before deleting the private stage and any provably uncommitted candidate. Canonical committed content is reconciled before every control mutation and survives discard. Publication receipts keep renamed-but-uncommitted trees charged across restart.

Own-job or administrator reads and controls require fresh current source ACL. Administrative controls do not acquire the original actor's commit identity. Resume and producer admission revalidate that actor's current admission/edit permissions, exact original descriptor and configured producer digest. Invalid authority/source/config becomes explicitly paused. A deliberate rebase changes only publication CAS expectation; completed pages, original source revision and extraction provenance remain fixed. A commit merges only the document representation into the current authorized item revision, preserving unrelated fields.

Stage page replies are explicitly provisional job output. They carry neither canonical content/revision identities nor citations. Once publication is committed, canonical page APIs supply the saved revision/document identity. A lost commit reply reconciles the immutable actor-principal operation receipt before cancellation, rebase or disposal, including administrator recovery after that account disappears.

Tiny fake-producer acceptance checks cover129 page outcomes,65 sequential completions, cancellation/fencing/restart, one uncertain page, changed replay, ACL and original-actor revocation, configuration/source changes, deliberate rebase, committed lost replies, interrupted archive/lookup and renamed-before-CAS quota/disposal. These verify lifecycle and structural processing coverage; they do not make a recognition accuracy claim or invoke models.
